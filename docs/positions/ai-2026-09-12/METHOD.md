# Review method: LLL and Base120

LLL is used here as a candidate design grammar: a ladder of evidence states,
a lattice of typed relationships, and a loop of recorded revision. It is not
claimed as a new scientific discipline, a mathematical proof of governance,
or a demonstrated improvement over simpler review methods.

- Ladder: distinguish an attributed report, bounded assessment, proposed policy,
  adopted company statement and published copy. Evidence for one state does not
  automatically establish the next.
- Lattice: a source supports, limits or contradicts a claim; a claim informs a
  position; a position constrains copy; an experiment tests a business hypothesis.
  This is a review vocabulary, not a proof that the graph is a mathematical lattice.
- Loop: retain a comparison baseline, identify contrary evidence, revise wording,
  preserve its scope and stop when the available evidence cannot justify a stronger claim.

## Base120 skill roles

The eight Base120 skills structure complementary review questions. Framework
use is a method choice, not evidence of intelligence, novelty or commercial effect.
The [public operator catalog](https://hummbl.io/models/) defines the available
operators; using a skill does not require applying every operator.

| Skill | Concrete review responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| base120 | Bind operator names to the catalog instead of inventing aliases |
| base120-perspect | State the audience, decision, assumptions and evidence cutoff |
| base120-invert | Seek counterexamples and identify when a claim must stop or narrow |
| base120-decompose | Separate capability, license, financing, provenance and economic propositions |
| base120-compose | Connect each position to atomic claims, sources, limitations and revision triggers |
| base120-recurse | Preserve corrections and comparison baselines; prevent evaluator changes from masquerading as progress |
| base120-systema | Align public wording, deployment controls, buyer acceptance and full costs |
| base120-infrastructure | Use consistent names and structured output so reviewers can inspect the assembled argument |

The decomposition is practical separability, not statistical independence.
The systems check asks whether each claim has an appropriate kind of evidence;
it does not claim to prove Ashby’s law for a real deployment. Receipt integrity
and successful local consistency checks remain distinct from semantic truth.

## Bounded improvement rule

A stronger improvement claim needs a defined outcome, baseline, evaluation
version, resource accounting and replication appropriate to its consequences.
Changed evaluators need versioned comparisons, not silent score substitution.
The commercial proposal measures downstream outcomes among eligible inquiries;
it does not infer demand creation from a sample that begins after inquiry.
