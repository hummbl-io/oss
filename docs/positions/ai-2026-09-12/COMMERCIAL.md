# Commercial translation: evidence that helps a buyer decide

Status: proposed experiment, not observed demand or profitability. The public
[services offer](https://hummbl.io/services) is the pricing reference observed
2026-09-12 (H-03). No customer demand, sale or profitability result is asserted by this proposal.

## The buying decision

Target an existing technical buyer deciding whether to deploy a hosted model,
self-host available weights, or retain the current workflow. The useful paid
question is: **Which option can complete this bounded job with acceptable
quality, cost, data handling and control, and what evidence supports that choice?**

AGI labels and IPO narratives may explain why the buyer is asking now, but they
are not the deliverable. The position paper gives HUMMBL a consistent answer;
the service supplies customer-specific evidence that a position paper cannot.

Use the existing Bridge Method Diagnostic/advisory → readiness audit → implementation sprint → retainer
path. Do not create another product line merely to package this research.
An initial advisory can qualify the decision and its missing evidence. An audit
can produce the evaluation and boundary map. A sprint can implement selected
controls. A retainer is warranted only by ongoing change and review work.

## Review-ready offer language

> Bring one agent workflow and the decision you need to make. We will identify
> the acceptance criteria, control boundaries and evidence gaps, then recommend
> a next step based on your constraints. If a comparison or implementation is
> needed, we scope it separately and preserve the results and their limits.

This wording promises a scoped advisory deliverable, not a guaranteed model
winner, compliance certification, AGI readiness, savings percentage or return.
It is proposed copy for the existing service, not a newly published offer.

## Before-sale scope gate

Before accepting payment, put one decision, the fixed price or spending cap,
included preparation and meeting time, and the promised artifact in the agreed
scope. The posted $400 Bridge Method Diagnostic is a 30-minute advisory session
with preparation and concrete next steps; the six-part audit below is separately
scoped at the applicable audit price.
Specify the number of alternatives and trials, permitted data routes, buyer
inputs and deadlines. Do not imply unlimited model testing or research.

Name the buyer's acceptance owner, acceptance criteria, review window and
included revision allowance. Agree how missing inputs, disputed acceptance or
additional work change the scope and price before performing that work. Apply
the actual agreed cancellation/refund terms and payment schedule; this paper
does not invent a new refund policy or contract. Verify that the labor budget
fits the collected price under realistic costs before accepting the engagement.

## Minimum deliverable for a scoped model-choice audit

1. One decision statement with owner, task distribution, deadline and budget.
2. A model/version/license and data-flow inventory. Record access restrictions,
   retention requirements, permitted operations and the fallback path.
3. A predeclared acceptance rubric and held-out task set representing the buyer's
   workflow. Keep prompts, model version, quantization, tools and budgets recorded.
4. Matched trials of the current baseline and feasible alternatives. Report
   failures, abstentions, latency, retries, reviewer time, and accepted outputs.
5. Total cost per accepted outcome and sensitivity to utilization and labor.
6. A recommendation, unresolved risks, reproducible receipt bundle, and a
   specific condition under which the recommendation should be reversed.

No sensitive customer data is needed to prepare the template. Actual trials
require an agreed scope and permitted data/model routes. Do not assume the
local workstation can host or retrain every available-weight model.

## Unit economics: illustrative sensitivity, not HUMMBL results

For a delivery, let P be collected revenue excluding pass-through taxes;
f the payment/collection fraction; A direct compute and other variable costs;
h all attributable labor hours; and r the fully loaded hourly opportunity cost.

`contribution = P × (1 − f) − A − h × r`

`contribution margin = contribution / P`

For target margin m:

`maximum hours = (P × (1 − f − m) − A) / r`

The following are **illustrative inputs**, not measured costs or a statement
of any payment provider's pricing. P uses the observed advisory price or the
lower end of the public service range. Assume f=3%, r=$100/hour and m=50%.

| Scenario | P | A | Assumed h | Contribution | Margin | Maximum h at 50% target |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Bridge Method Diagnostic | $400 | $5 | 2 | $183 | 45.75% | 1.83 |
| Readiness audit | $5,000 | $50 | 25 | $2,300 | 46.00% | 23.00 |
| Implementation sprint | $15,000 | $150 | 80 | $6,400 | 42.67% | 69.00 |

These are contribution scenarios, not net profit: fixed overhead, acquisition
expense, taxes, bad debt and unused capacity still matter. Substitute actual
effective collection fees, refunds and costs for these illustrative inputs.
Define what the loaded labor rate includes and allocate each cost once; do not
add the same overhead again under another heading. Track founder time
even when no payroll cash leaves the business. A labor-saving claim must not
omit added review, incident or migration work. For self-hosting, allocate hardware,
power, storage and operations at observed utilization; weights being downloadable
does not make those costs zero.

## One bounded experiment

**Hypothesis BIZ-002:** among eligible inbound inquiries, a source-bound
model-choice explanation improves downstream qualification/conversion and
reduces avoidable delivery rework compared with HUMMBL's current explanation.
Because enrollment begins after an inquiry arrives, this design does not test
whether the copy increases traffic or attracts more inbound demand.

**Before exposure:** freeze the current copy, the proposed copy above, the
qualification rubric, attribution window and eligible audience. Select the audience and agree distribution before launch. The proposed
measurement design does not itself change a checkout or public funnel.

**Primary metric:** contribution per eligible assigned opportunity after a
common follow-up window, including qualification time and opportunities that
did not buy. Freeze eligibility and assignment before exposure to either copy;
do not let the new copy change its own denominator by filtering who qualifies.
**Secondary metrics:** qualification rate, qualified-to-paid conversion, acceptance of the scoped
deliverable, preparation/delivery/rework hours, refunds and repeat work.
**Do not substitute:** impressions, generated posts, page views or meeting count
for collected revenue or delivery contribution.

Suggested enrollment window: the next ten eligible opportunities or 30 days,
whichever comes first. Follow every assigned opportunity for 60 days from its
assignment date before the primary comparison. Record delivery completion,
collection, refunds and unresolved obligations at that cutoff; report immature
or unfinished opportunities separately without dropping them from the denominator.
For prepaid or unfinished work, label the 60-day cash contribution provisional
and report remaining delivery hours, refund exposure and other obligations
alongside it. A positive cash balance before work is complete does not establish
a profitable delivery. Report final delivery contribution when the obligations
are resolved; do not silently compare one arm's completed work with the other's
unearned prepayments.
An interim review can stop harmful copy but cannot declare final conversion or
profit from completed deliveries alone. These are proposed operating bounds, not statistically
justified sample sizes. Small counts cannot establish a causal lift. Preserve
channel, buyer maturity, task difficulty and referral status; if assignment is
not randomized, call the comparison observational. Compare with the baseline
under similar conditions and report uncertainty, including no-sale outcomes.

**Stop or revise:** a prospect interprets the copy as a capability/compliance
guarantee; a source supporting the promise becomes false; delivery exceeds its
agreed budget; or attributable costs erase the expected contribution. Do not
add unpriced research to rescue a poorly scoped engagement.

**Continue only if:** the buyer receives and accepts the bounded deliverable,
the work produces positive contribution under recorded inputs, and no material
claim failure remains. A first successful engagement supports that engagement;
it does not establish a general ROI promise. Do not advertise a savings number
until evidence supports that exact population, timeframe and cost boundary.

## Proposed measurement sheet

This is an unfilled template for the experiment, not customer data or a launch.
Adapt it before exposure; changes after exposure need a dated
protocol amendment and cannot silently remove already assigned opportunities.

A workable initial eligibility rule is a new inbound inquiry about a concrete
agent/model deployment decision, with no existing paid scope for that same
decision. Treat duplicate inquiries about one decision as one opportunity.
Record any channel, geography or data-route exclusions before enrollment.
Budget, decision authority, available inputs and deadline can be qualification
results after exposure; failing them does not remove an eligible assignment.
Use the same qualification rubric for both copy versions. Freeze a random
assignment procedure if a randomized comparison is intended; otherwise record
the actual assignment rule and retain the observational label.

| Field group | Minimum record and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Enrollment | Minimally identifying opportunity ID, eligibility decision/time, duplicate check, channel and pre-exposure assignment rule |
| Exposure | Copy version, claim/source version, assignment timestamp, comparison arm; record non-exposure or crossover without deleting the assignment |
| Qualification | Decision owner present, bounded job identified, permitted inputs available, budget/timeline fit; each yes/no/unknown with the same rubric |
| Scope | Agreed price/cap, artifacts, alternatives/trials, acceptance owner/window, included revisions, cancellation/refund terms reference |
| Outcome | No sale, pending, delivery in progress, accepted, disputed or cancelled; date and reason without inferring acceptance from silence |
| Costs | Qualification, preparation, delivery, review, rework and support hours; loaded rate; direct compute/other costs; actual collection fees |
| Money | Cash collected, refunds, outstanding collection, remaining delivery/refund obligations; distinguish provisional cash from resolved-delivery contribution |
| Follow-up | Assignment plus 60 days, status at that date, later resolution recorded separately; preserve every assigned opportunity in the denominator |

For a comparison arm, divide the sum of its recorded opportunity contributions
by **all eligible opportunities assigned to that arm**, including no-sale costs.
Do not average only the margins of engagements that sold. Report the underlying
counts, cash and remaining obligations alongside any average. Missing cost or
follow-up records are unknown, not zero; flag an incomplete comparison rather
than silently imputing profitability.

## Loop back into the position record

Capture a minimally identifying opportunity ID, offer version, source version,
decision scope, acceptance outcome, actual time/costs, collected revenue and
reason for loss or rework. Store customer records in the appropriate private
system, not in this paper. Publish only approved aggregates with denominators
and limitations. If the record changes a position, preserve the old statement,
new statement, evidence and decision owner in the change ledger.

The commercial effect remains unmeasured until paid engagements and their
full costs are observed under the declared protocol.
