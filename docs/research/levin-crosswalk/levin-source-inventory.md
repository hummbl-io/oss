## Constructs

### 1. Technological Approach to Mind Everywhere (TAME)

- **Source:** LEVIN-TAME-2022
- **Attribution posture:** `ATTRIBUTED_TERM` — Levin-authored framework name.
- **Definition (paraphrase):** An experimentally-grounded framework for
  understanding diverse bodies and minds, including non-standard and
  synthetic agents, using a multi-scale competency architecture.
- **Antecedents:** Cybernetics (Wiener, Ashby), autopoiesis
  (Maturana/Varela), enactivism (Varela/Thompson), basal cognition
  literature (Lyon et al.).
- **Ownership note:** The acronym TAME is Levin-associated. The general
  project of understanding diverse minds is not unique to Levin.

### 2. Multi-scale competency architecture

- **Source:** LEVIN-TAME-2022, LEVIN-TALK-2026
- **Attribution posture:** `GENERAL_SCIENTIFIC_CONCEPT` — the phrase is
  Levin-associated but the idea of competencies at multiple scales has
  antecedents in hierarchical control, modularity, and multi-level selection.
- **Definition (paraphrase):** A system in which locally competent
  components at different scales compose into a larger agent without
  requiring unified consciousness or identity at every level.
- **Antecedents:** Hierarchical control systems, subsumption architecture
  (Brooks), swarm intelligence, multi-level selection theory.

### 3. Cognitive light cone

- **Source:** LEVIN-TAME-2022, LEVIN-TALK-2026
- **Attribution posture:** `ATTRIBUTED_TERM` — the specific phrase appears
  Levin-associated. The general concept of bounded cognitive scope has
  antecedents.
- **Definition (paraphrase):** The set of spatial, temporal, organizational,
  and moral variables an agent can represent, protect, and trade off. Agents
  with larger light cones can reason over longer horizons and broader scopes.
- **Antecedents:** Markov blankets (Friston), temporal horizons, viable
  systems model (Beer), hierarchical agency, sensorimotor contingencies.

### 4. Basal cognition

- **Source:** LEVIN-TAME-2022 (references); broader literature
- **Attribution posture:** `GENERAL_SCIENTIFIC_CONCEPT` — the term predates
  Levin's specific usage. Lyon, Keijzer, and others use it in plant
  cognition and cellular cognition contexts.
- **Definition (paraphrase):** Cognitive processes (learning, memory,
  decision-making, problem-solving) in systems without brains, including
  single cells and synthetic systems.
- **Antecedents:** Plant cognition, cellular cognition, enactivism,
  autopoiesis, cybernetics.

### 5. Anatomical homeostasis and morphogenetic goal states

- **Source:** LEVIN-TAME-2022, LEVIN-PEZZULO-2026
- **Attribution posture:** `ATTRIBUTED_TERM` — "anatomical homeostasis" and
  "morphogenetic goal state" are Levin-lab-associated formulations.
- **Definition (paraphrase):** The tendency of biological systems to
  maintain or restore specific anatomical configurations despite
  perturbation, treating morphology as a setpoint-driven control problem.
- **Antecedents:** Homeostasis (Cannon), morphogenetic fields, bioelectric
  signaling, regeneration biology.

### 6. Developmental bioelectric coupling

- **Source:** LEVIN-TAME-2022, LEVIN-PEZZULO-2026
- **Attribution posture:** `ATTRIBUTED_TERM` — Levin-lab program.
- **Definition (paraphrase):** Bioelectric signaling as the mechanism by
  which local cellular competencies scale into larger-scale anatomical
  goals, enabling collective problem-solving in morphogenetic space.
- **Antecedents:** Bioelectricity, gap junction signaling, developmental
  biology, pattern formation.

### 7. Nested or composite agency

- **Source:** LEVIN-TAME-2022
- **Attribution posture:** `GENERAL_SCIENTIFIC_CONCEPT` — the idea of nested
  agency has antecedents in hierarchical agency, subsumption, and
  multi-agent systems.
- **Definition (paraphrase):** An agent composed of competent sub-agents,
  where the composite exhibits capabilities the parts lack, without
  requiring the parts to "know" they are participating in a larger agent.
- **Antecedents:** Subsumption architecture (Brooks), hierarchical
  task networks, multi-agent systems, stigmergy.

### 8. Perturbation and goal persistence

- **Source:** LEVIN-TAME-2022
- **Attribution posture:** `GENERAL_SCIENTIFIC_CONCEPT` — goal persistence
  under perturbation is a standard control-theory and cybernetics concept.
- **Definition (paraphrase):** The capacity of a system to maintain or
  restore its goal state after disturbance, used as evidence of agency.
- **Antecedents:** Homeostasis (Cannon), control theory, viability theory,
  resilience engineering.

### 9. Persuadability spectrum

- **Source:** LEVIN-TAME-2022, LEVIN-TALK-2026
- **Attribution posture:** `ATTRIBUTED_TERM` — Levin uses "persuadability"
  in a specific bioelectric-intervention sense.
- **Definition (paraphrase):** The degree to which a system's behavior can
  be altered by non-destructive intervention (molecular, bioelectric,
  communicative) rather than by force or component replacement. Interventions
  can be routed by the least invasive layer that reliably restores governed
  behavior.
- **Antecedents:** Pharmacology, neuromodulation, persuasion in rhetoric,
  control theory (input selection).

### 10. Disease as altered scope, coupling, memory, or target-state behavior

- **Source:** LEVIN-TAME-2022, LEVIN-TALK-2026
- **Attribution posture:** `ATTRIBUTED_TERM` — Levin's specific framing of
  cancer and disease as agency-boundary breakdowns.
- **Definition (paraphrase):** Disease (including cancer) as a state where
  the collective's goal scope narrows, coupling between levels breaks,
  memory of the target anatomical state is lost, or the target-state
  behavior shifts to a lower-scale default.
- **Antecedents:** Cancer as atavism, cancer as breakdown of tissue
  cooperation, disease as homeostatic failure.

### 11. Novel synthetic or hybrid beings

- **Source:** LEVIN-TAME-2022, LEVIN-TALK-2026
- **Attribution posture:** `GENERAL_SCIENTIFIC_CONCEPT` — the category is
  not unique to Levin but his framework explicitly addresses it.
- **Definition (paraphrase):** Future or constructed agents (biological,
  synthetic, or hybrid) whose cognitive and moral status is unfamiliar,
  requiring new frameworks for ethical treatment and governance.
- **Antecedents:** Bioethics, AI ethics, moral status of non-human animals,
  synthetic biology ethics.

### 12. Moral-status and treatment uncertainty for unfamiliar agents

- **Source:** LEVIN-TAME-2022, LEVIN-TALK-2026
- **Attribution posture:** `GENERAL_SCIENTIFIC_CONCEPT` — moral status
  uncertainty is a standard bioethics topic.
- **Definition (paraphrase):** The ethical and practical challenge of
  deciding how to treat agents whose cognitive capacity, substrate, or
  origin does not fit existing moral categories.
- **Antecedents:** Bioethics, animal ethics, AI ethics, personhood theory.
