# Prior Art And Adjacent Taxonomies

**Status:** Draft v0.1
**Last Updated:** 2026-05-26

## Purpose

This document records adjacent classification systems and how HUMMBL's
ANI/ASPI/AGI/ASI taxonomy differs.

This is not a complete literature review. Claims that require exhaustive search
are marked `UNVERIFIED`.

## DeepMind Levels Of AGI

Google DeepMind's "Levels of AGI" framework classifies AI progress using
performance depth and generality breadth.

HUMMBL uses this as adjacent prior art because it treats generality as a
dimension rather than a binary threshold.

Difference:

- DeepMind levels are a progress-measurement framework.
- HUMMBL tiers are governance-changing operating categories.
- ASPI names the domain-deep, bounded-autonomy deployment tier between narrow
  tools and domain-general systems.

Source: `docs/references.md`.

## General-Purpose AI

The EU AI Act uses general-purpose AI terminology for regulatory obligations.

HUMMBL treats GPAI as a regulatory category, not an intelligence tier.

Difference:

- GPAI asks what regulatory obligations attach to a model category.
- HUMMBL taxonomy asks what capability scope and governance posture a system has.

Source: `docs/references.md`.

## NIST AI RMF

NIST AI RMF is a risk-management framework, not an intelligence taxonomy.

HUMMBL uses NIST-style risk framing as a governance companion, not as a tier
definition.

Source: `docs/references.md`.

## Domain-Specific AI

Domain-specific AI describes a system design or specialization strategy.

ASPI is different because it is a capability-and-governance tier:

- domain-specific AI can still be narrow,
- ASPI requires domain breadth, novelty handling, transfer, and different
  governance requirements.
