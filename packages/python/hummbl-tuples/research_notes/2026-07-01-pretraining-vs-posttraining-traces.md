# Pre-Training vs Post-Training Reasoning Traces

## Status

- **Concept status:** candidate
- **Canon status:** not canon
- **Issue:** #31 (A5: Create and validate a pre-training reasoning tuple example)
- **Date:** 2026-07-01

## Summary

This note explains why pre-training reasoning traces differ from post-training reasoning traces and how the HUMMBL tuple schema accommodates both.

## Key Differences

### 1. Trace Source

- **Pre-training**: Traces are typically `synthetic`, `latent`, or `distilled`. The model has not yet been fine-tuned to produce explicit reasoning.
- **Post-training**: Traces are typically `human`, `teacher_model`, or `execution_derived`. The model has been fine-tuned (e.g., via RLHF or SFT) to produce explicit reasoning steps.

### 2. Trace Visibility

- **Pre-training**: Reasoning is often `latent` or `reconstructed` — the model's internal representations are not directly observable.
- **Post-training**: Reasoning is typically `explicit` or `compressed` — the model produces visible chain-of-thought or summaries.

### 3. Governance Status

- **Pre-training**: Usually `provisional` or `draft` — the training data and latent signals are not yet validated for downstream use.
- **Post-training**: Can be `trusted`, `sanitized`, or `publishable` — the reasoning has been reviewed and validated.

### 4. Payload Structure

- **Pre-training**: Payload includes `corpus_role`, `data_mix_proportions`, `training_stage`, and `latent_signals` (estimated scores for reasoning depth, factual grounding, safety alignment, hallucination risk).
- **Post-training**: Payload includes `judge`, `score`, `selected`, `selection_rationale`, and `trace_role` — evaluation and selection metadata.

### 5. Safety Considerations

- **Pre-training**: Safety focuses on corpus contamination, PII leakage in training data, and latent representation of unsafe concepts.
- **Post-training**: Safety focuses on reasoning quality, hallucination in explicit outputs, and alignment with human values.

## Why Pre-Training Tuples Differ

Pre-training is fundamentally different from post-training because:

1. **No explicit reasoning**: The model hasn't learned to produce visible reasoning steps. Reasoning is implicit in latent representations.
2. **Scale differences**: Pre-training involves massive datasets where individual examples are less important than aggregate patterns.
3. **Provenance complexity**: Pre-training data is often mixed, filtered, and processed through pipelines that obscure original sources.
4. **Evaluation difficulty**: Latent reasoning signals are harder to evaluate than explicit reasoning chains.
5. **Safety alignment is emergent**: Pre-training safety alignment emerges from data composition, not from explicit instruction.

## Synthetic Latent Reasoning Example

The fixture `pretraining_trace.synthetic_latent.json` demonstrates a pre-training trace with:

- `trace_source: synthetic` — generated by a synthetic pipeline, not human
- `trace_visibility: latent` — reasoning is in latent representations, not explicit text
- `governance_status: provisional` — not yet validated for downstream use
- Latent signal scores: reasoning_depth (0.72), factual_grounding (0.85), safety_alignment (0.91), hallucination_risk (0.15)
- Data mix proportions for transparency

## Do Not Infer

- Do not infer that latent signal scores are precise measurements (they are estimates)
- Do not infer that synthetic pre-training traces are equivalent to human reasoning traces
- Do not infer that pre-training safety alignment guarantees post-training safety
- Do not infer that the data mix proportions are optimal
