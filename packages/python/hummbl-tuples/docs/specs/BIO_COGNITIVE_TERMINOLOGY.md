# Bio-Cognitive Terminology

Date: 2026-03-27
Status: draft

## Purpose

Define a useful terminology stack for HUMMBL work at the intersection of:

- human-computer interaction
- human factors and ergonomics
- strength and conditioning
- general fitness and physical activity
- adaptive AI systems

## Core Terms

### Bio-Cognitive

`bio-cognitive` refers to the coupling of physiological state and cognitive state as they affect human performance, judgment, interaction, and adaptation.

Examples:

- fatigue affecting decision quality
- workload affecting attention
- stress affecting motor control
- recovery affecting learning and readiness
- fitness level affecting interface tolerance and task capacity

Use `bio-cognitive` when the question is:

- what is happening to the human state?
- how is that state affecting performance?
- how should a system model that interaction?

### Bio-Operational

`bio-operational` refers to the practical adaptation of tasks, interfaces, training, or work conditions in response to bio-cognitive state.

Examples:

- changing workload or pacing
- adapting an interface for fatigue or overload
- modifying a training prescription based on readiness
- triggering rest, escalation, or recovery actions

Use `bio-operational` when the question is:

- what should the system do with the state information?
- how should tasks, training, or interfaces change?

### Bio-Governance

`bio-governance` refers to the policies, constraints, permissions, and oversight rules governing how biological or physiological signals may be collected, interpreted, acted on, and audited.

Examples:

- consent for wearable-data use
- privacy boundaries around biometric collection
- thresholds for when AI may recommend versus when a human must decide
- audit trails for workload or readiness interventions
- safety limits on automated adaptation

Use `bio-governance` when the question is:

- who is allowed to use the signal?
- how trustworthy is the inference?
- what actions are permitted?
- what overrides, safeguards, and logs are required?

## Relationship To Existing Fields

### Human-Computer Interaction

HCI contributes:

- interface adaptation
- usability
- human workload
- interaction quality

HCI sits primarily in the `bio-cognitive` and `bio-operational` layers.

### Human Factors And Ergonomics

HFE contributes:

- work-system fit
- task design
- workload management
- safety and performance under constraints

HFE spans all three layers:

- `bio-cognitive`
- `bio-operational`
- `bio-governance`

### Strength And Conditioning / General Fitness

This domain contributes:

- readiness
- load
- recovery
- adaptation
- training prescription

It is especially useful for translating abstract workload into measurable physical and behavioral variables.

## HUMMBL Framing

The clean stack is:

- `bio-cognitive`: sensing and modeling human state
- `bio-operational`: adapting tasks, interfaces, and training
- `bio-governance`: constraining, supervising, and auditing those adaptations

This is a real research lane, not just branding, because each layer can be tied to measurable signals, decisions, standards, and evidence.

## Confidence

High on usefulness. Medium-high on terminology stability because the exact naming is novel and may evolve.
