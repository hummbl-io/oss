# Knowledge Management Notes

Date: 2026-03-27
Status: draft

## Question

What do knowledge management and organizational memory add to HUMMBL?

## Bottom Line

Knowledge management gives HUMMBL a direct bridge from reasoning artifacts to organizational memory. It explains how tacit and explicit knowledge move through people, systems, routines, and communities of practice, and why memory can be an asset or a liability.

For HUMMBL, this matters because tuples, Base120/BaseN, and shared reasoning traces are not just outputs. They are candidates for organizational memory, retrieval, transfer, and reuse.

## Core Ideas

- tacit and explicit knowledge circulate differently and need different support
- organizational memory is distributed across people, routines, roles, documents, and systems
- communities of practice are a major channel for knowledge transfer
- codification alone is not enough; retrieval, interpretation, and reuse matter
- memory can constrain, enable, or distort future action

## What HUMMBL Should Borrow

- treat tuples as candidates for organizational memory, not just coordination exhaust
- distinguish tacit and explicit knowledge in system design
- design for retrieval, interpretation, and reuse rather than only storage
- support communities of practice and shared repertoires around Base120/BaseN
- track when memory is updated, contested, or deprecated

## What HUMMBL Should Avoid

- assuming codification alone preserves useful knowledge
- confusing archival accumulation with actual organizational learning
- treating all knowledge as equally transferable across contexts
- ignoring tacit knowledge and situated expertise
- building memory without governance for obsolescence, drift, or misuse

## Relation To HUMMBL

  Can function as shared repertoires and interpretive assets inside communities of practice.
- tuples / governance
