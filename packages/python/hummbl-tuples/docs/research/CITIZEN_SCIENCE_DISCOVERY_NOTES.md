Status: draft

## Question

Who is arguably the most prolific citizen scientist ever, and who deserves honorable mention?

## Bottom Line

If forced to pick one, the strongest answer is probably **William Herschel**.

That is an inference, not a formal title. The case is that he began outside the professional scientific establishment, discovered Uranus, and compiled catalogs totaling about 2,500 nebulae and clusters with Caroline Herschel's help. That is unusually strong both in scale and in scientific importance.

Primary sources:

- Britannica on William Herschel  
  https://www.britannica.com/biography/William-Herschel
- Britannica historical survey of nebulae, noting William Herschel's three catalogs totaling about 2,500 objects  
  https://www.britannica.com/science/nebula/Historical-survey-of-the-study-of-nebulae

## Honorable Mention

The strongest honorable mention is **Caroline Herschel**.

Reasons:

- discovered eight comets
- played a central role in survey and catalog work
- is one of the clearest examples of a non-institutional or marginal-to-institutional scientist making enduring discoveries

## Why This Matters

The lesson is not just that amateurs can contribute.

The deeper pattern is:

- discovery often comes from patient review of large archives
- outsider persistence matters
- tooling shifts can open new discovery windows
- institutional scarcity creates space for well-organized individuals

That is exactly why astronomy is attractive now for AI-assisted citizen discovery.

## Confidence

Medium. The category is inherently subjective because "citizen scientist" spans centuries, disciplines, and changing definitions of professional science.
