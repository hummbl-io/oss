## Question

Why does Herbert Simon matter for HUMMBL?

## Bottom Line

Simon is the cleanest classic source for HUMMBL's decision-system framing. His core move is that real decision-makers operate under bounded rationality: incomplete information, limited computation, and organizational constraints.

For HUMMBL, that maps directly onto Base120 and BaseN as a reasoning-control layer over constrained agents and organizations. Simon keeps the system honest by shifting attention from ideal optimization to actual process, search, aspiration levels, and satisficing.

## Core Ideas

- bounded rationality is rational action under information, time, and computation limits
- decision systems should be studied as processes, not only outcomes
- search matters because alternatives are often not given in advance
- satisficing matters because agents stop when they find acceptable solutions, not global optima
- organizations reduce complexity through subgoals, specialization, authority, and communication structures
- good theories should be grounded in how people actually decide

## What HUMMBL Should Borrow

- treat reasoning as a process with explicit search, stopping rules, and aspiration levels
- model organizations as decision systems with subgoals, roles, and communication paths
- prefer process artifacts that capture how a decision was reached, not only the final answer
- use satisficing explicitly for bounded contexts where full optimization is unavailable or too costly
- keep Base120 and BaseN as a disciplined procedural layer, not a claim of perfect rationality

## What HUMMBL Should Avoid

- overstating optimization when the real system is bounded and path-dependent
- collapsing decision quality into final outcome quality alone
- assuming the best policy is always the globally optimal one
- treating organizational structure as cosmetic rather than computational
- using reasoning traces as decoration instead of process evidence

## Relation To HUMMBL

- Base120 / BaseN  
  Strong fit as a catalog of procedural reasoning operators, stopping rules, and search heuristics.
- tuples / governance  
  Very strong fit for encoding decisions, subgoals, aspiration levels, search branches, and evidence of satisficing.
- world models / reasoning traces  
  Simon is more about process and problem representation than predictive world models, but his focus on problem spaces maps well to reasoning traces.
- readiness / BKI / mission-governed operation  
  Strong fit because readiness is about whether the organization can actually decide and act under constraints, not whether it has perfect information.
