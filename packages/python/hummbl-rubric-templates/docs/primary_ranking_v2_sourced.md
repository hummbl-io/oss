## 1. Executive Summary & Epistemic Methodology

This document delivers the empirical re-evaluation of **19 primary global AI governance organizations, regulatory bodies, standards institutions, and multilateral initiatives**. 

Under the HUMMBL **LLL-C10 Verification-State Honesty Protocol**:
1. **Zero False Precision:** Every awarded score across the 7 calibrated axes is directly grounded in empirical indicators defined in `rubric_v2_calibrated.md`.
2. **Dual Primary Citation Rule:** Every score claim is substantiated by $\ge 2$ verifiable primary sources (formal statutes, treaties, published audit evaluations, regulatory orders, or parliamentary proceedings).
3. **No Uncalibrated Scores:** No qualitative assertions or evaluative marketing claims are accepted without institutional receipts.
4. **The Governance Trilemma Finding:** No organization achieved Tier 1 ($\ge 80/100$). The global governance landscape exhibits a structural trilemma: institutions possess at most two of **(a) Binding Statutory Teeth**, **(b) Frontier Technical Depth**, or **(c) Multilateral Jurisdictional Reach**.

## 3. Detailed Organization Profiles & Primary Evidence Dossiers

### 1. European AI Office (European Commission) — Score: 78/100 (Tier 2)
- **Mandate:** Central enforcer of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 across the EU single market; oversees general-purpose AI (GPAI) models with systemic risk and coordinates national market surveillance authorities.
- **Axis Breakdown:**
  - *TEA (20/25):* Level 4. Direct statutory authority to issue fines up to €35M or 7% of annual worldwide turnover (Art. 99); power to request unredacted documentation, conduct on-site evaluations, and mandate mitigation measures.
  - *FTC (14/20):* Level 4. Scientific office staffed with machine learning researchers; scientific panel advising on systemic risks; access to EuroHPC supercomputing infrastructure.
  - *SRL (14/15):* Level 4. Complete prohibited AI practices list (Art. 5: social scoring, real-time biometric identification, cognitive behavioral manipulation); mandatory systemic risk containment tripwires.
  - *IIC (12/15):* Level 4. Civil service administrative independence within the European Commission; multi-stakeholder advisory forum; subject to European Ombudsman oversight.
  - *AVR (7/10):* Level 4. Delegated act update procedures for technical annexes; annual review mechanisms; constrained by multi-stage EU legislative processes.
  - *TRA (7/10):* Level 4. Public registry of high-risk AI systems; published codes of practice; proprietary commercial models protected under trade secret clauses.
  - *GJC (4/5):* Level 4. Comprehensive single-market coverage across 27 EU member states with significant extraterritorial "Brussels Effect" on international developers selling into the EU.
- **Primary Citations:**
  1. *Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 June 2024 laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence (Artificial Intelligence Act)*, Official Journal of the European Union, OJ L 1689, 12.7.2024, pp. 1–144. [Articles 5, 51–56, 64–70, 99–101].
  2. *Commission Decision of 24.1.2024 establishing the European Artificial Intelligence Office*, C(2024) 390 final, Official Journal of the European Union.

## 4. Systemic Findings & The Governance Trilemma

### 4.1 The Trilemma Decomposition
The empirical evidence across all 19 organizations proves that the global AI governance landscape is constrained by a fundamental trilemma:

```
                  [Statutory Teeth]
                     (EU, CAC)
                       /   \
                      /     \
                     /       \
                    /         \
  [Frontier Technical Depth] ---- [Multilateral Reach]
       (UK AISI, US AISI)             (UN, UNESCO, OECD)
```

1. **Teeth + Reach without Depth:** Treaties and multilateral agreements (e.g. Council of Europe CETS 225, UN AIAB) possess broad international legitimacy but lack sovereign compute or technical evaluation harnesses to verify frontier model activations directly.
2. **Depth + Teeth without Reach:** National regulatory bodies (e.g. EU AI Office, CAC) possess legally binding fine or shutdown powers, but their jurisdiction is geographically bounded, creating persistent risks of regulatory arbitrage in havens.
3. **Depth + Reach without Teeth:** Technical consortiums and advisory institutes (e.g. UK AISI, ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42, FMF) develop sophisticated technical benchmarks and enjoy international adoption, but operate purely on voluntary compliance or self-certification.
